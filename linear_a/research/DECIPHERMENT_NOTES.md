# Linear A Decipherment Notes

## Current State of Knowledge

Linear A remains undeciphered despite over a century of scholarly effort. Here we document our analysis and hypotheses.

## What We Know For Certain

### 1. The Script
- Syllabic writing system (like Linear B)
- ~90 core syllabic signs, plus ideograms and numerics
- Written left-to-right
- Word dividers used (vertical lines or spaces)
- Many signs are shared with (derived from) Cretan Hieroglyphic
- Linear B was derived from Linear A

### 2. The Language (Minoan)
- **NOT Greek** - When Linear B values are applied, the results are not Greek
- **NOT Indo-European** (probably) - No clear cognates found
- Possibly a language isolate
- May be related to "Eteocretan" (later non-Greek language of Crete)

### 3. Context
- Used ca. 1800-1450 BCE
- Administrative tablets (lists, inventories, totals)
- Religious inscriptions (libation tables, sanctuary offerings)
- Vessel inscriptions (ownership? contents?)

### Key Vocabulary

#### Confirmed
- **pa-i-to** (AB03-AB28-AB05): Phaistos - confirmed via Linear B

#### High Confidence
- **ku-ro** (AB77-AB26): "Total/sum" - appears at end of lists
- **ki-ro** (AB67-AB02): May be variant or related term

#### Probable Divine Names
- **ja-sa-sa-ra-me-ne** / **a-sa-sa-ra-me-ne**: Religious formula
  - Appears on libation tables
  - Possibly a goddess name/epithet
  - Compare: Semitic "Asherah"? But no proven connection

- **i-da-ma-te**: Appears in sanctuary texts
  - i-da: possibly "of Ida" (Mt. Ida in Crete)
  - ma-te: compare Greek "mater" (mother)?
  - Could be precursor to Demeter worship

- **a-ta-i-*301-wa-ja**: Opening of libation formulas
  - Invocation formula
  - Meaning unknown

### Grammatical Patterns

#### Word Structure
- Average word length: 3-4 signs
- Consistent with CV syllabary writing
- Suggests typical word length of 3-4 syllables

#### Possible Suffixes
Signs appearing frequently word-finally:
- **-te** (AB04): Very common ending - case marker? verb ending?
- **-na** (AB06): Frequent - possible feminine/collective?
- **-i** (AB28): Vowel ending - locative? dative?
- **-a** (AB08): Vowel ending - nominative?

#### Possible Prefixes/Articles
Signs appearing frequently word-initially:
- **a-** (AB08): Very common - article? prefix?
- **ja-** (AB57): Frequent in religious texts

### Numeric System
- Vertical strokes: units (1)
- Dots: tens (10)
- Circles: hundreds (100)
- Circle with center dot: thousands (1000)
- Fractional signs: J (1/2), E (1/4), F (1/8), K (1/16)

## Decipherment Attempts

### What Doesn't Work

1. **Greek readings**: Applying Linear B values produces non-Greek results
2. **Semitic connections**: Some proposed, none convincing
3. **Indo-European**: No systematic correspondences found
4. **Anatolian (Luwian, Hittite)**: Some proposed, controversial

### Promising Approaches

1. **Internal analysis**: Study patterns within Linear A itself
2. **Semantic fields**: Use ideograms to identify vocabulary domains
3. **Administrative formulas**: Compare with Linear B administrative texts
4. **Place names**: Anchor points where names survive into Greek

## Obstacles to Decipherment

1. **Small corpus**: Only ~1,400 inscriptions
2. **No bilingual**: Unlike Egyptian (Rosetta Stone), no translation key
3. **Unknown language family**: Can't use comparative method effectively
4. **Limited text types**: Mostly administrative lists, few narratives
5. **Sign variation**: Regional and temporal variants complicate analysis

## What Would Help

1. **New inscriptions**: Especially longer texts or new text types
2. **Bilingual text**: A text in Linear A and a known language
3. **Related language**: Discovery of related language would help
4. **Better chronology**: Understanding script evolution

## Conclusion

Linear A remains one of the great unsolved puzzles of ancient writing. While we can **read** many signs (using Linear B values), we cannot **understand** the underlying Minoan language.

The most promising avenue is continued internal analysis:
- Identifying word patterns and grammar
- Mapping semantic fields via ideograms
- Using place names as anchor points
- Statistical analysis of sign distributions

A breakthrough may come from:
- New archaeological discoveries
- Identification of a related language
- Machine learning pattern recognition
- Interdisciplinary analysis combining linguistics, archaeology, and computer science
